The growth of data is commonly described in terms of volume, variety, and velocity. Structured data  follows a fixed schema and is typically stored in rows and columns, such as data in relational  databases. Semi-structured data has some organizational structure but does not conform to a  rigid schema, such as JSON or XML files. Unstructured data has no predefined structure, such  as text documents, images, audio, and video [5].  The ability of a storage architecture to accommodate these different data types is one of the  key factors that differentiates traditional approaches, such as the Da ta Warehouse, from more  flexible approaches, such as the Data Lake and Data Lakehouse [5][1].  2.2 Basic Components of Data Storage Architecture

Data typically flows through a storage architecture in a sequential manner: it is first collected  from source systems, then ingested into the storage environment, processed or transformed as  required, and finally made available for consumption by end users or downstream applications.  This flow may occur in batch mode, where data is processed at scheduled intervals, or in  streaming mode, where data is processed continuously as it arrives.  The specific manner in which this flow is implemented, and the degree of transformation  applied before storage, varies significantly between different architectural paradigms, as  discussed in Chapter 3 [8][4][6].  2.4 Basic Architecture  The basic structure of a Data Storage Architecture can be represented as a sequence of layers  connecting data sources to end users, as illustrated in Figure 2.1 [8][9].
